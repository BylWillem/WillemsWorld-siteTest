A note on the rare extreme: the “abuse, neglect, or unsafe home” slice includes rare but real cases of unregulated custody transfer (also called “rehoming”) — where adoptive parents transfer children to strangers via online forums without oversight. A 2013 Reuters investigation identified roughly 5,000 cases over 5 years, with 70% involving internationally-adopted children. What the comparison reveals
Adoption produces life outcomes that track close to the general population on education, employment, and incarceration — and dramatically better than the alternative. The gap that remains is in mental health: adopted adolescents are 2–4 times more likely to attempt suicide than peers raised by biological parents, even when their adoptive families provide significant material and emotional resources. The most defensible explanation isn't adoption itself, but the cumulative effect of pre-adoption experience — loss, instability, and in many cases early trauma — carried into a new family. Adoption mitigates these effects substantially. It does not erase them.

Sources for incarceration: Bureau of Justice Statistics general-population baseline; Juvenile Law Center and Foster Care Statistics for foster alumni incarceration rates; Font et al., “From foster care to incarceration” — adopted children showed lower risk than aged-out peers, similar to general population. Employment: Bureau of Labor Statistics; Northwest Foster Care Alumni Study — median income ~$14,000 at age 25 for foster alumni. Suicide attempts: Slap et al., Pediatrics (2001) — 7.6% of adopted adolescents vs 3.1% of non-adopted (n=6,577); Keyes et al. (2013), Pediatrics — OR 4.23 for suicide attempts among adoptees in Minnesota study; 2020 meta-analysis — pooled RR ~3.0 across cohort studies; Casey Mental Health Outcomes and Northwest Alumni Study (2006) — 6–11× elevated suicide attempt risk for foster alumni. Adopted-children outcomes are aggregates across domestic, international, and foster-to-adopt pathways; outcomes vary substantially by age at adoption and pre-adoption experience.
