By age

Most abortion patients aren't teenagers

Cultural framing often imagines abortion as something teenagers do. The actual distribution is older. Roughly 9 in 10 abortion patients are 20 or over, and the median age is around 27. More than half are already mothers seeking to limit family size.

Repeat abortions

U.S. abortion patients · prior abortion history
About half of abortions are someone's first

A common assumption is that repeat abortions are rare. They aren't. Among abortion patients, roughly 1 in 2 has had at least one prior abortion. This proportion has remained stable since 1993, suggesting the pattern reflects contraceptive failure and access friction rather than any cultural shift.

Why this matters
A repeat abortion isn't typically a failure of intention — it's most often a failure of contraceptive method. Women with prior abortions are more likely to be using a less-effective method like condoms or withdrawal, often because of cost or access barriers to better options. This is the same income/effectiveness pattern Section 04 explores in depth: the cheapest contraceptive options fail most often, and the women using them are most likely to need an abortion when they do.

Sources: Jones & Singh, “Repeat Abortion in the United States,” Guttmacher Institute; Jones, Jerman & Ingerick, “Which Abortion Patients Have Had a Prior Abortion?” (Journal of Women's Health, 2018) — based on 2014 APS, n=8,380; CDC Abortion Surveillance 2015 — 43.6% with 1+ prior abortion. Guttmacher and CDC report slightly different repeat-abortion rates; the consensus range is 44–50% with 1+ prior. Repeat-abortion proportion has been stable since the early 1990s per Guttmacher longitudinal analysis.

The geography

As of March 2026
Legal status by state
Total ban. Abortion is illegal at any stage. Narrow exceptions exist (life of mother, sometimes rape/incest) but are difficult to use in practice. 13 states.
6–12 week ban. Abortion is illegal once cardiac activity is detected (~6 wks) or after 12 wks — often before many people know they are pregnant. 7 states.
15–22 week limit. Abortion is legal earlier in pregnancy but banned after a fixed gestational age (15, 18, or 22 weeks). 3 states.
Viability limit. Abortion is legal until fetal viability (~24–26 wks); after that, only with medical exception. 19 states.
No gestational limit. No state-imposed cutoff; later abortions are governed by clinical judgment. 9 states & D.C.

Source: Guttmacher Institute, State Bans on Abortion Throughout Pregnancy (April 2026); cross-referenced with KFF, Center for Reproductive Rights, and Pew Research Center state law trackers (March 2026).
